MagentaDude Audio, or MDA for short, is a simple lossy audio format optimized for pleasant quality, predictable ~100kbps compression, and blazing-fast encoding and decoding.

Q&A

How does MDA compare to [insert audio format here]?

MDA and many other audio formats are lossy, which means that at lower bitrates they have artifacts that cause noticable—and for some listeners, unpleasant—changes to the audio. For example, MP3 can sound metallic, especially at lower bitrates, and Ogg Vorbis can sound airy.

While MDA definitely can have audible artifacts, its artifacts aim to sound more natural, like white noise or vinyl, giving a better listening experience.

In addition, MDA is a simpler format than many others, making it faster to decode and using less battery life.

What if I don't want any artifacts at all?

For that you would want a lossless audio format. I recommend the excellent and widely supported FLAC format, which typically cuts the size of an uncompressed WAV file in half while still preserving all audio data.
